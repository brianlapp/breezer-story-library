# The Envelope

The first letter arrived on a Tuesday, which was rude of it.

Mara had arranged her Tuesdays very carefully. Tea at seven. Bread at eight. Absolutely no surprises before lunch. But there it was on the mat, a blue envelope addressed in her own handwriting.

Inside was a single sentence:

> On Sunday, when the rain stops, leave the window open.

She read it three times. Then she put the kettle on, because mysteries were easier to consider with something warm in her hands.

All week, the letter sat beside the fruit bowl. On Wednesday she decided it was a joke. On Thursday she checked the weather. On Friday she found herself dusting the windowsill.

Sunday arrived wearing clouds.

Mara waited.

At four minutes past three, the rain stopped. The street outside shone like someone had varnished it. She opened the window.

A paper aeroplane sailed in and landed neatly in her tea.

It was folded from a page of the book she had never finished writing.

On the dry corner, in handwriting that was almost hers, it said:

**You still have time.**

Mara fetched a towel. Then a pen.
