# The Lost & Found

At eleven forty-seven, the laundromat coughed up a constellation.

It happened between the spin cycle and the part where Nell remembered she had forgotten the detergent. A small blue star rolled out of dryer number six, bounced twice on the linoleum, and came to rest beside her left shoe.

“Oh,” she said.

The star made a noise like a teaspoon tapping a glass.

Nell looked around. The laundromat was empty except for a fern, three abandoned magazines, and a man asleep under a newspaper dated next Thursday. Outside, rain freckled the windows. The moon hung unusually low over the car park, as though it had misplaced something.

She picked up the star. It was warm. It smelled faintly of toast.

Pinned to its smallest point was a receipt.

> ONE SMALL WISH  
> Paid in full.  
> No refunds after sunrise.

Nell turned it over. On the back, in tiny, impatient handwriting, someone had written: **Please stop putting these in the tumble dryer.**

She had questions. Several, in fact. But before she could decide which one deserved to go first, the fern cleared its throat.

“You'll want the lost and found,” it said.

“Of course,” said Nell, because sometimes the only reasonable response to an unreasonable evening is to be very polite.

The fern pointed one leaf toward a door she was almost certain had not been there before.

On the door was a brass plate:

**THINGS PEOPLE MEANT TO DO.**

Below it, a smaller sign:

**Please knock. Some of them are sleeping.**
