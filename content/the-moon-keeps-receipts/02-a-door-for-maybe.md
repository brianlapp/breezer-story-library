# A Door for Maybe

Nell knocked with the hand that wasn't holding a star.

“Come in,” said a voice. “Unless you're an apology. We close for apologies at ten.”

The room beyond was far larger than the laundromat. Shelves climbed into a violet haze. On them sat hundreds of glass jars, each with a handwritten label: *Learn Italian*. *Call Dad*. *Take the long way home*.

Behind a desk made of mismatched drawers sat a woman with silver eyebrows and a cardigan the colour of marmalade.

“I'm here about this,” Nell said, presenting the star.

The woman sighed. “Another pocket wish. People never check their pockets.”

“Is it mine?”

“Do you remember wishing for it?”

Nell thought about all the things she had wanted that week. A quieter upstairs neighbour. A holiday. The courage to wear the yellow coat hanging unworn by her front door.

“I don't know,” she admitted.

“That's usually a yes.”

The woman opened a drawer and removed a thimble. From the thimble she pulled a ladder. From the ladder she unhooked a tiny brass key.

“This opens a maybe,” she said. “Just the one. Use it before breakfast.”

“And if I don't?”

“Then it becomes a probably not.”

Nell closed her fingers around the key. Behind her, somewhere in the ordinary world, the dryer beeped.

For the first time all evening, she knew exactly what to do next.

She would put on the yellow coat.
